> You perform a sequence of `PUSH` and `POP` operations on a stack whose size
> never exceeds $k$. After every $k$ operations, a copy of the entire stack is
> made automatically, for backup purposes. Show that the cost of $n$ stack
> operations, including copying, is $\O(n)$ by assigning suitable amortized
> costs to the various stack operations.

To do that, we assign:

* `PUSH` to 3
* `POP` to 1

The key thing here, is that the stack size never exceeds $k$, which is both the
maximum size of the stack, and the number of operations between copies. Using
the accounting method, `PUSH` pays for three things:

* The actual push operation
* A subsequent pop operation that could happen later
* A bit of the $k$ price of the copy

As for `POP`, it is already prepaid, but it also pays for the copy operation in
the future as well.

This way, whenever the copy comes, the full price for it is paid. Even if we
maintain an almost full stack between copies, alternating a push and a pop,
making a copy of the full stack would be already paid for when the time comes.

Since both costs are constant, the total cost of the $n$ operations is $\O(n)$.
