## The cost of restructuring red-black trees

> There are four basic operations on red-black trees that perform **structural
> modifications**: node insertions, node deletions, rotations, and color
> changes. We have seen that `RB-INSERT` and `RB-DELETE` use only $\O(1)$
> rotations, node insertions, and node deletions to maintain the red-black
> properties, but they may make many more color changes.
>
> 1. Describe a legal red-black tree with $n$ nodes such that calling
>    `RB-INSERT` to add the $(n+1)$st node causes $\Omega(\lg n)$ color changes.
>    Then describe a legal red-black tree with $n$ nodes for which calling
>    `RB-DELETE` on a particular node causes $\Omega(\lg n)$ color changes.
>
> Although the worst-case number of color changes per operation can be
> logarithmic, you will prove that any sequence of `RB-INSERT` and `RB-DELETE`
> operations on an initially empty red-black tree causes $\O(m)$ structural
> modifications in the worst case.
>
> <ol start="2">
>   <li>Some of the cases handled by the main loop of the code of both
>   <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code> and <code>RB-DELETE-FIXUP</code> are
>   <strong>terminating</strong>: once encountered, they cause the loop to
>   terminate after a constant number of additional operations. For each of the
>   cases of <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code> and <code>RB-DELETE-FIXUP</code>,
>   specify which are terminating and which are not. (<em>Hint:</em> Look at
>   Figures 13.5, 13.6, and 13.7 in Sections 13.3 and 13.4)
> </ol>
>
> You will first analyze the structural modifications when only insertions are
> performed. Let $T$ be a red-black tree, and define $\Phi(T)$ to be the number
> of red nodes in $T$. Assume that one unit of potential can pay for the
> structural modifications performed by any of the three cases in
> `RB-INSERT-FIXUP`.
>
> <ol start="3">
>   <li>Let $T'$ be the result of applying Case 1 of
>   <code>RB-DELETE-FIXUP</code> to $T$. Argue that $\Phi(T') = \Phi(T) - 1$.
>   <li>We can break the operation of <code>RB-INSERT</code> procedure into
>   three parts. List the structural modifications and potential changes
>   resulting from lines 1-16 of <code>RB-INSERT</code>, from nonterminating
>   cases of <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code>, and from terminating cases of
>   <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code>.
>   <li>Using part (d), argue that the amortized number of structural
>   modifications performed by any call of <code>RB-INSERT</code> is $\O(1)$.
> </ol>
>
> Next you will prove that there are $\O(m)$ structural modifications when both
> insertions and deletions occur. Define, for each node $x$,
>
> $$
>  w(x) =
>    \begin{cases}
>      0 & \text{if $x$ is red}, \\\\
>      1 & \text{if $x$ is black and has no red children}, \\\\
>      0 & \text{if $x$ is black and has one red child}, \\\\
>      2 & \text{if $x$ is black and has two red children}.
>    \end{cases}
> $$
>
> Now redefine the potential of a red-black tree $T$ as
>
> $$ \Phi(T) = \sum_{x \in T} w(x) $$
>
> and let $T'$ be the tree that results from applying any nonterminating case of
> `RB-INSERT-FIXUP` or `RB-DELETE-FIXUP` to $T$.
>
> <ol start="6">
>   <li>Show that $\Phi(T') \le \Phi(T) - 1$ for all nonterminating cases of
>   <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code>. Argue that the amortized number of structural
>   modifications performed by any call of <code>RB-INSERT-FIXUP</code> is
>   $\O(1)$.
>   <li>Show that $\Phi(T') \le \Phi(T) - 1$ for all nonterminating cases of
>   <code>RB-DELETE-FIXUP</code>. Argue that the amortized number of structural
>   modifications performed by any call of <code>RB-DELETE-FIXUP</code> is
>   $\O(1)$.
>   <li>Complete the proof that in the worst case, any sequence of $m$
>   <code>RB-INSERT</code> and <code>RB-DELETE</code> operations perform $\O(m)$
>   structural modifications.
> </ol>
