> Redo Exercise 16.1-3 using a potential method of analysis.

I am struggling with the formalism in this one.

First, it's worth noting that the cumulative cost of each operation that is an
exact power of $2$ is $3n - \lg n - 2$. This is not relevant for my solution,
but I calculated it, and hence I can include it here.

Anyway, we can define the potential to be "the number of operations since the
last power of 2, times 2". The change of potential is then simple to calculate.
For operations that are not powers of two, it simply increases by three, thus:

$$
  \hat c = 1 + \Phi(n) - \Phi(n-1) = 1 + 2 = 3
$$

When $n$ is an exact power of $2$, where would have been $n/2 - 1$ operations
before that that were not a power of two. Thus:

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \hat c &= n + \Phi(n) - \Phi(n-1) \\\\
         &= n + 0 - 2 \big( \frac n 2 - 1 \big) \\\\
         &= n - n + 1 \\\\
         &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

In both cases, the amortized costs are constant, thus the whole sequence is
linear.
